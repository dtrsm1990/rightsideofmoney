Volatility Like You've Never Seen

The stock market drops. Crypto collapses, recovers, and collapses again. Know the history before you join it.

On Day 1 you learned what crypto is. Today you learn what it does to the people who own it. If you've invested in the stock market, a 20% drop feels like a crisis. In crypto, a 20% drop can happen in a weekend, and nobody even calls it news.

Bitcoin's Largest Declines

PeriodPeak to BottomApproximate DeclineWhat Was Happening
2011About $32 to about $2About 93%Early, tiny market and an exchange hack
2013 to 2015About $1,150 to about $170About 85%The Mt. Gox exchange collapse
2017 to 2018About $19,700 to about $3,200About 84%The 2017 bubble bursts
2021 to 2022About $69,000 to about $15,500About 77%Rising interest rates, the Terra collapse, and FTX
2025 to 2026 (so far)About $126,000 to about $59,000About 53%Tariff shock, leverage unwinding, and a long slide

Bitcoin is the steadiest major crypto asset. Smaller coins usually fall further in the same downturns, often 90% or more, and many never come back.

When It Happens Fast

  • March 2020: Bitcoin fell about 40% in a single day, and roughly half its value over two days, as the pandemic hit.
  • October 10, 2025: A tariff announcement sparked the largest forced selling in crypto history. More than $19 billion in borrowed positions were wiped out and over 1.6 million trading accounts were liquidated in about a day.

The Math of Losses

Losses and gains aren't symmetrical. If something falls 50%, it has to rise 100% just to get back to even. If it falls 80%, it needs a 400% gain.

Real-Life Case Study: Renee, Two Cycles

Renee bought $2,000 of bitcoin near the top in late 2021. A year later it was worth about $460. She didn't sell, because the money was a small slice of her savings and she'd decided in advance she could live with losing it. By the end of 2024 it was worth more than she'd paid.

Her coworker Brandon put $15,000 of his house fund into the same coin at the same time. When it fell 77%, he needed the money and sold for about $3,400. Same asset. One person sized for the storm. The other didn't. (Recovery isn't guaranteed. Many coins never recovered at all.)

Right Side of Money Principle

Don't ask "how much could I make?" Ask "could I hold this through an 80% drop without it changing my life?" If the answer is no, the position is too big.

When Platforms Fail: The Lessons Others Paid For

Some of crypto's biggest losses had nothing to do with price. People trusted the wrong company.

When you buy crypto on an app, you usually don't hold the crypto yourself. The company holds it for you. That's called custody, and it means your money is only as safe as that company. This is called counterparty risk, and it has cost everyday people billions.

Five Failures Every Crypto Owner Should Know

EventWhat HappenedThe Lesson
Mt. Gox (2014)Once the world's largest bitcoin exchange, it collapsed after roughly 850,000 bitcoin were lost or stolen. Customers waited about ten years for partial repayment.Even the biggest platform can fail.
Terra (May 2022)An "algorithmic" stablecoin lost its $1 peg and its sister token went to nearly zero within days."Stable" is a claim, not a guarantee. Ask what backs it.
Celsius (2022)A lending platform offering high yields froze withdrawals and filed for bankruptcy. A judge ruled that coins in its yield accounts belonged to the company, not the customers.Read the terms. In some accounts, you're a lender, not an owner.
FTX (November 2022)One of the most popular exchanges collapsed after customer money was secretly used by an affiliated trading firm. Its founder was convicted of fraud and sentenced to 25 years.Celebrity ads and big sponsorships prove nothing.
Bybit (February 2025)Hackers the FBI attributed to North Korea stole about $1.5 billion in ether, the largest crypto theft ever recorded.Security failures happen even at large platforms.

The Protections You Don't Have

ProtectionCovers Bank Deposits?Covers Stocks and Funds at a Brokerage?Covers Crypto?
FDIC insuranceYes, up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per categoryNoNo
SIPC protectionNoYes, up to $500,000 if the brokerage failsNo, unless the asset is a registered security, such as an ETF

That last row matters. A spot bitcoin ETF held in a brokerage account is a registered security, so it falls under the protections of the brokerage system. Bitcoin itself held on a crypto exchange does not. Day 4 compares these options side by side.

Five Questions to Ask Any Platform

  1. Who actually holds my crypto, and is it kept separate from the company's own money?
  2. What do the terms say happens if the company goes bankrupt?
  3. Is it licensed to operate in my state, and by which regulator?
  4. Does it publish proof that its reserves cover customer balances, verified by an outside firm?
  5. Can I withdraw my crypto to my own wallet whenever I want?

Right Side of Money Principle

A crypto platform isn't a bank, even when its app looks like one. Trust is earned with proof, not with advertising.

Day 2 | Lesson 3

Leverage, Yield, and Other Ways to Lose Money Fast

The fastest losses in crypto rarely come from buying and holding. They come from the add-ons.

Crypto platforms make much of their money from products that feel exciting and sound sophisticated: leverage, futures, high-yield "earn" accounts, and new token launches. These are where beginners get hurt the fastest.

Leverage: Borrowed Money, Borrowed Time

Leverage means trading with borrowed money. With 10x leverage, $100 controls $1,000 worth of crypto. Sounds powerful, until you do the math the other way.

LeveragePrice Move That Can Wipe Out Your MoneyHow Often Bitcoin Moves That Much
2xAbout a 50% dropSeveral times in its history
5xAbout a 20% dropMany times a year in rough markets
10xAbout a 10% dropSometimes in a single day
50x to 100xAbout a 1% to 2% dropConstantly

When a leveraged position loses enough, the platform automatically sells it. That's a liquidation, and it usually happens at the worst possible moment. On October 10, 2025, more than 1.6 million accounts were liquidated in a single day. Many of them were people who'd been "right" about the long-term direction and still lost everything because of a few hours.

"Earn" Accounts and High Yields

When a platform offers 8%, 12%, or 20% a year on your crypto, ask the most important question in finance: where does the money come from? Usually your crypto is lent out to traders or invested in risky strategies. If those go bad, you can lose your deposit. Celsius and BlockFi both offered high yields before freezing customer withdrawals in 2022.

Staking

Staking means locking up coins to help run a proof-of-stake network, in exchange for rewards. It's real, and it's built into how those networks work. But know the trade-offs:

  • Your coins may be locked up for days or weeks, so you can't sell during a crash.
  • Rewards are paid in the same coin, so a 4% reward means little if the price falls 50%.
  • Staking rewards are generally taxable as income when you receive them.
  • If you stake through a platform, you take on that platform's risk too.

Presales, Airdrops, and "Get In Early"

New tokens are often sold with promises of getting in before the public. Insiders typically hold large shares and unlock them later, selling to the people who bought the hype. A pump-and-dump works the same way: promoters hype a coin, the price jumps, and they sell to the late buyers.

Real-Life Case Study: Marcus and the 25x Trade

Marcus had been buying bitcoin slowly for a year and was up about $1,800. A trading influencer convinced him to "make it work harder" with 25x leverage. He put $1,000 into a leveraged position. Bitcoin dropped 4.5% overnight while he slept. He woke up to a liquidation notice. The $1,000 was gone.

A week later bitcoin was higher than when he'd opened the trade. He was right about the direction and still lost everything, because leverage doesn't give you time to be right.

Right Side of Money Principle

For beginners, the rule is simple: no leverage, no borrowed money, no yields you can't explain, and no "get in early" offers. Boring is how you stay in the game long enough to win.

Day 2 | Lesson 4

How Much Is Too Much: Sizing Your Position

The most important crypto decision you'll make isn't which coin. It's how much.

Professional investors spend more time on how much to own than on what to own. Position sizing is what turns a terrifying crash into an uncomfortable month. Get this right and almost nothing in crypto can ruin you.

Step 1: Foundation First

Before any money goes into crypto, these should be in place:

  1. An emergency fund of at least three months of essential expenses, in an insured savings account.
  2. No high-interest debt. Paying off a 24% credit card is a guaranteed 24% return. Crypto guarantees nothing.
  3. Your retirement basics. At minimum, you're getting any employer match on your workplace plan.
  4. Money you won't need for at least five years. Crypto can stay down for years.

Step 2: Size for the Worst Case

There's no single right number, and this program won't give you a personal recommendation. But here's how many educators and institutions frame it:

  • Many financial educators suggest that people who choose to own crypto keep it to a small slice of their investable money, often in the low single digits as a percentage.
  • In late 2024, BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager, described a 1% to 2% bitcoin allocation as a reasonable range for portfolios that choose to include it.
  • Whatever the percentage, the real test is the same: could you lose 80% of it and keep living your life exactly as planned?

Position Size Calculator

Investable money means savings beyond your emergency fund and beyond anything you'll need in five years.

Step 3: Size Within Crypto

Even inside your crypto slice, not all coins carry the same risk. Many cautious investors keep the large majority of their crypto in the largest, longest-running networks and treat anything smaller as a very small, high-risk portion they're fully prepared to lose. Meme coins and new launches are the riskiest positions anyone can take.

Step 4: Write It Down

A limit in your head disappears the moment prices take off. Write your number down, today, while you're calm. Your Day 2 Action Exercise does exactly that.

Real-Life Case Study: Two Approaches, Same Crash

Tonya and Greg each had $30,000 in investable savings in 2021. Tonya put 3% ($900) into crypto. Greg put 40% ($12,000) into crypto after watching it rise.

In the 2022 crash, both lost about 75% of their crypto. Tonya lost about $675, roughly 2% of her savings, and kept going. Greg lost about $9,000, nearly a third of his savings, sold in a panic, and swore off investing for three years, missing the stock market recovery entirely. The crash was the same. The size decided the damage.

Right Side of Money Principle

Size every crypto position so that if it went to zero, you'd be disappointed, not devastated.
