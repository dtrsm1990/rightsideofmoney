Module 2 Assessment

Mindset and Fear Assessment

This assessment measures the five forces that keep capable people from acting on what they know: avoidance, shame, fear, paralysis, and self-criticism. You'll get a profile of which ones are active, where they likely came from, and a specific plan to break the strongest one. Plan for 25 to 35 minutes.

Participant Information

Your report is prepared under this name and sent to this email address and to Darrell.

Part 1: Knowledge Section

Five scenario questions covering Module 2. You need 80% (4 of 5) to unlock Module 3.

1Marcus has an unopened letter from his auto lender that's been sitting on the counter for three weeks. What does Module 2 teach about the cost of leaving it unopened?

2Which response reflects the Observer rather than the Judge?

3Shame and guilt affect behavior differently. What did June Tangney's research generally find?

4Kiara wants to start investing but has spent a year comparing apps, reading reviews, and waiting until she "understands everything." Which Unfreeze Protocol step would help most right now?

5What's the main purpose of the Weekly Money Meeting?

Part 2: Mindset and Fear Diagnostic

Twenty statements. Choose how often each one is true for you right now.

1.I put off opening bills, statements, or financial mail.
2.I don't know the current balance of at least one of my accounts or debts.
3.I change the subject when money comes up in conversation.
4.I review my accounts on a regular schedule.
5.I feel embarrassed about my financial situation.
6.I hide purchases, balances, or debts from people close to me.
7.I believe my financial situation says something bad about who I am as a person.
8.I compare my finances to other people and feel like I'm behind.
9.When I think about money, I imagine worst-case outcomes.
10.I feel physical anxiety (tight chest, racing heart, upset stomach) when dealing with finances.
11.I worry about money even when my bills are paid.
12.I avoid financial opportunities because I'm afraid of losing money or making the wrong choice.
13.I know what I should do with my money but can't make myself start.
14.I wait for the "right time" or the perfect plan before taking financial action.
15.I research financial decisions for so long that I never actually decide.
16.When a financial task feels big, I break it into small steps and start.
17.I call myself names or speak harshly to myself about money mistakes.
18.One financial mistake makes me feel like my whole plan is ruined.
19.I replay past financial mistakes over and over in my mind.
20.I can look at a financial mistake with patience and figure out what to do next.

Part 3: Written Reflection

Your answers are included in your report and sent to Darrell.

Name the item and the fear directly. Minimum 30 words.

Use numbers where you can. Minimum 30 words.

Write it as if you're speaking to them directly. Minimum 40 words.

When you submit, your personal report appears on this page and a copy is sent to Darrell Thompson. Take your time. Honest answers produce an accurate report. There are no right answers in the diagnostic sections, only true ones.
