Level 2 · Module 2 · Week 6 · Lesson 2.2

Risk Isn't the Enemy. Ignorance Is.

You can't avoid risk and build wealth. You can know exactly how much you can survive.

This week: Teaching, Mindset Drill, Worksheets, Daily Proof Log, Operator Log, Check-In With Darrell

Two Ways to Get Risk Wrong

Some people avoid all risk. They keep every dollar in a checking account, and inflation quietly takes a piece of it every year. Others take risks they don't understand, chasing returns with money they can't afford to lose. Both are driven by the same thing: not knowing their real risk limit.

Risk isn't the enemy. Unmeasured risk is.

Three Kinds of Risk Tolerance

TypeThe Question
Risk capacityHow much can you lose without damaging your bills, your reserve, or your family? This is math.
Risk needHow much risk do your goals actually require? Sometimes it's less than you think.
Risk temperamentHow much can you watch fall in value without panicking and selling? This is behavior.

Your real tolerance is the lowest of the three. Plenty of people have the capacity for risk and the temperament of someone who sells at the bottom.

The Loss Survivability Test

Before any money goes into anything that can lose value, run this test in writing:

  1. What's the total amount at risk?
  2. If it dropped 30% and stayed down for two years, what would happen to your bills, your reserve, and your plan?
  3. If you lost all of it, what would happen?
  4. Would you be forced to sell anything else, borrow, or change your lifestyle?

If the honest answer to the third question is "I'd be in trouble," the amount is too big, no matter how good the opportunity looks.

Time Horizon Changes Everything

Money you'll need within a few years shouldn't be exposed to large swings, because you might be forced to sell at a loss. Money you won't touch for a decade or more can generally ride out more volatility. Match the risk to the timeline, not to your mood.

This is education, not personal investment advice. If you want guidance on your specific situation, work with a licensed professional who's required to act in your interest.

The Standard

You don't need to fear risk. You need to know your limit before the market tests it for you.

Mindset Drill

The Risk History Review

Look at how you've actually behaved around risk, not how you think you'd behave. This review becomes part of your Week 6 proof. Your answers save on this device as you type and are sent with your Operator Log.

Field Assignment

Do the Work

  1. Complete your Risk History Review.
  2. Write your Personal Risk Tolerance Definition, including the dollar amount you could lose without damage.
  3. Run the Loss Survivability Test on everything you currently own that can lose value, and on any investment you're considering.
  4. Start your Risk Awareness Log today and keep it every day through Checkpoint 2.
  5. Keep your Asset Awareness Log, and hold your Weekly Operator Review.
