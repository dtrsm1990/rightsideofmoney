Welcome to the Challenge

30-Day Mindset & Money Challenge

Thirty days. One day at a time. By Day 30 you'll know where your money goes and why, you'll talk to yourself about money differently, and you'll have automatic savings running without needing willpower.

A Message From Darrell

When I joined the Marine Corps in 1988, nobody handed me a financial roadmap. I learned one habit at a time, and I learned that small disciplines repeated every day beat big plans that fade by Friday. That's what this challenge is. It isn't about perfection. It's about showing up for 30 days and letting small actions change who you are with money. Do the work, one day at a time, and you'll be amazed at who you are on Day 30.

Darrell Thompson, Founder, Right Side of Money

Your Three Daily Tools

  • The 48-Hour Spending Tracker: Every week you log every purchase for 48 hours, including how you felt and what drove it. Your tracker builds a live summary of your spending patterns.
  • Money Affirmations: Every week you write or refine an affirmation using the RSM formula, rate how believable it is, and pair it with a proof action.
  • Money Moves: Every week you take one concrete action: your first automatic transfer, then leveling it up, a sinking fund, and a plan for how it grows.

How Each Day Works

  1. Read the day's lesson or exercise.
  2. Take the action step.
  3. Pass the knowledge check on lesson days (80% required).
  4. Complete the Day Check-In: check your habits, rate your mood, and name one win.

At the end of each week, a Progress Check pulls in your tracker, affirmation, money move, and check-ins, then sends you and Darrell a personal report that identifies your primary pattern and gives you a plan for the next week.

Your 30-Day Calendar

Challenge Rules

  1. One day at a time, in order.
  2. Log every purchase during your 48-hour tracking windows, especially the small ones.
  3. Never miss twice. On hard days, do your minimum day.
  4. Never cancel a transfer. Lower it if you need to.
  5. Be honest. Your reports are only as accurate as your answers.
