Module 7

Protecting Your Wealth

Building wealth without protecting it is like filling a bucket with a hole in the bottom.

The Core Truth

Most people spend years building wealth and almost no time protecting it. Then one medical crisis, one lawsuit, one premature death, or one uninsured disaster erases everything. The same discipline you apply to building your financial life has to be applied to protecting it. Protection isn't pessimism. It's the final piece of a complete financial plan.

The Protection Stack

Financial protection operates in layers. No single policy or plan covers everything. But together, the right coverage creates a fortress around everything you've built and everything you're building.

Layer 1: Emergency Fund (Already Covered in Module 5)

Your emergency fund is your first line of protection. Before any insurance kicks in, before any policy pays out, your cash reserve is what keeps a disruption from becoming a disaster. This is why it's non-negotiable. Review Module 5 if yours isn't funded yet.

Layer 2: Health Insurance

Medical debt is the leading cause of personal bankruptcy in America. One serious illness or accident without health coverage can generate bills that take decades to pay off, wiping out everything you've built. Health insurance isn't optional if you have any assets to protect.

Key terms to understand when choosing health coverage:

  • Premium: What you pay each month for the coverage, whether you use it or not.
  • Deductible: What you pay out of pocket before insurance starts covering costs.
  • Copay: A fixed amount you pay per visit or service.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum: The most you'll pay in a year, after which insurance covers 100%. This is your financial exposure ceiling. Know this number.
  • HSA (Health Savings Account): If you have a high-deductible health plan, an HSA lets you save money tax-free for medical expenses. Unused funds roll over and can be invested. It's one of the best tax advantages available.

Layer 3: Life Insurance

If anyone depends on your income, you need life insurance. Full stop. This isn't about morbid thinking. It's about making sure the people you love don't lose their financial stability along with you.

There are two primary types. Know the difference:

  • Term life insurance: Coverage for a specific period, typically 10, 20, or 30 years. It pays out if you die during the term. It's affordable, straightforward, and what most financial advisors recommend for the majority of people. A healthy 35-year-old can get $500,000 of coverage for around $25-40 per month.
  • Whole life / permanent life insurance: Coverage that lasts your entire life and includes a cash value component. Significantly more expensive than term. Often sold as an investment vehicle, but the returns are generally inferior to simply buying term insurance and investing the premium difference in index funds. Be cautious here.

How much coverage do you need? A common rule of thumb is 10-12 times your annual income. Factor in your outstanding debts (especially the mortgage), your income replacement need, childcare and education costs if you have children, and your spouse's ability to maintain the household financially.

Layer 4: Disability Insurance

Your most valuable financial asset isn't your savings account. It's your ability to earn income. If you're 35 and earn $60,000 per year, and you work until 65, your future earning potential is $1.8 million plus raises and growth. Disability insurance protects that asset.

The statistics on this are sobering: 1 in 4 workers will experience a disability that keeps them out of work for 90 days or more during their career. Yet only a fraction of workers have private disability coverage.

  • Short-term disability: Covers a portion of your income for a few weeks to six months. Many employers offer this.
  • Long-term disability: Covers a portion of your income (typically 60-70%) for years or until retirement if you're unable to work in your occupation. Employer-provided long-term disability coverage is often not enough on its own. Look into supplemental coverage.

Layer 5: Auto and Property Insurance

If you own a car, you're legally required to carry auto insurance in nearly every state. But required minimum coverage often isn't enough to protect your assets. If you cause an accident and the damages exceed your liability coverage, your personal assets can be targeted in a lawsuit. The right coverage depends on the value of your vehicle and your net worth.

Homeowners or renters insurance protects your possessions and provides liability coverage if someone is injured on your property. Renters insurance is one of the most overlooked protections available, and it typically costs less than $20 per month. If you're renting and you don't have it, get it today.

Layer 6: Estate Planning

Estate planning isn't just for the wealthy. It's for anyone who has assets, dependents, or preferences about what happens when they're gone. Without basic estate documents, the state decides what happens to everything you've built, who gets your children, and who makes medical decisions if you can't.

The basic documents everyone should have:

  • Will: Specifies who gets your assets and, critically if you have minor children, who becomes their guardian. Without a will, the court decides. That might not match what you wanted.
  • Beneficiary designations: Review the beneficiary designations on your retirement accounts, life insurance policies, and bank accounts. These designations override whatever your will says. An outdated beneficiary designation from a previous marriage or relationship can have unintended consequences.
  • Durable power of attorney: Designates someone to manage your finances if you become incapacitated.
  • Healthcare proxy / medical power of attorney: Designates someone to make medical decisions on your behalf if you can't make them yourself.
  • Living will / advance directive: Documents your wishes regarding end-of-life care so your loved ones don't have to guess.

Building Generational Wealth Through Protection

Here's what most people miss about protection: it's not just about you. It's about the people who come after you. Generational wealth, the ability to pass something meaningful to your children and grandchildren, requires intentional protection.

When parents have adequate life insurance, their children don't have to interrupt their education or take on debt to survive. When estate plans are in place, assets transfer efficiently without probate eating the estate. When beneficiary designations are current, assets go directly to the right people without delay.

The generation before you may not have had access to this knowledge. You do. The protection you put in place today is a gift to everyone who comes after you, even if they never fully understand what you protected them from.

Your Protection Audit

Answer these five questions right now. Each "no" is an action item: (1) Do you have health insurance with a known out-of-pocket maximum? (2) Do you have life insurance if anyone depends on your income? (3) Do you have disability insurance beyond what your employer provides? (4) Do you have a current will with a designated guardian if you have minor children? (5) Have you reviewed beneficiary designations on all retirement accounts and life insurance in the last two years?
