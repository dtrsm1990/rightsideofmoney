Module 4 Assessment

Spending Psychology Assessment

This assessment identifies which of five spending drivers has the most influence over your money, calculates your personal convenience premium in dollars and life hours, and gives you a targeted plan to break your strongest pattern. Plan for 25 to 35 minutes.

Participant Information

Your report is prepared under this name and sent to this email address and to Darrell.

Part 1: Knowledge Section

Five scenario questions covering Module 4. You need 80% (4 of 5) to unlock Module 5.

1Why do digital wallets and one-click checkout tend to increase spending?

2A site says "Add $12 more to get free shipping (a $5.99 value)." What's the Builder response?

3Jada notices her spending spikes on paydays and right after she finishes big projects at work. Which driver is most likely, and what's the best matched strategy?

4Luis takes home $20 an hour and spends $360 a month on delivery. How many life hours does that represent?

5According to Gollwitzer's research, why do if-then plans work?

Part 2: Spending Driver Diagnostic

Twenty statements. Choose how often each one is true for you.

1.I shop or spend after a stressful day to feel better.
2.When I feel overwhelmed, buying something gives me a sense of control.
3.My spending goes up during stressful periods at work or home.
4.I have non-spending ways to calm down that I use consistently.
5.I browse shopping apps or sites when I have nothing else to do.
6.Scrolling social media leads me to buy things I wasn't looking for.
7.I shop late at night or during downtime out of habit.
8.Buying new things is one of my main sources of excitement.
9.I tell myself "I deserve this" or "I earned this" before buying something unplanned.
10.My spending increases on or right after payday.
11.After I stick to my budget for a while, I overspend to celebrate.
12.I have a planned amount for rewards that I stay within.
13.I order delivery or takeout because I didn't plan a meal.
14.I pay extra for speed or ease without thinking about the total cost.
15.I make convenience store or grab-and-go stops that I could have avoided with planning.
16.I plan meals, errands, and purchases ahead to avoid paying convenience premiums.
17.I spend more when I'm around certain friends, coworkers, or family.
18.I buy things partly because of how they'll look to other people.
19.I feel pressure to keep up with what people around me have or do.
20.I say yes to trips, dinners, or events I can't afford because I don't want to say no.
