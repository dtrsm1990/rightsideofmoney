Module 1 Assessment

Foundation Knowledge Assessment

This assessment finds the root beneath your financial symptoms. Part 1 confirms your understanding. Part 2 diagnoses which of four root causes is most active in your life. Part 3 puts your own story into words. Plan for 25 to 35 minutes.

Participant Information

Your report is prepared under this name and sent to this email address and to Darrell.

Part 1: Knowledge Section

Eight scenario questions covering all three Module 1 lessons. You need 80% (7 of 8) to unlock Module 2.

1Shawn earns $92,000 and feels broke every month. His coworker earns $61,000 and has a growing emergency fund and retirement account. According to the Stagnation Equation, what most likely explains the difference?

2Which of these is a root, rather than a symptom or a behavior?

3Nia gets a $4,000 bonus and feels unsettled until it's spent. Which concept explains this?

4Which money identity is most associated with spending on appearance, like cars, clothes, and vacations shared on social media?

5Research on money scripts by Dr. Brad Klontz and colleagues suggests what about our core money beliefs?

6Andre notices he shops online most often after arguments with his partner. The shopping makes him feel better for about an hour. Which emotional driver is most likely at work?

7What is 'urge surfing'?

8Which change would most directly address the 'No Operating System' part of the Stagnation Equation?

Part 2: Root-Cause Diagnostic

Sixteen statements. Choose how often each one is true for you. Answer based on what you actually do, not what you wish you did.

1.When I think about money, I hear phrases or rules I learned growing up.
2.I feel uncomfortable or guilty about wanting more money.
3.I believe people with my background aren't really meant to build wealth.
4.I have consciously questioned and rewritten the money beliefs I grew up with.
5.When my income goes up, my spending rises to match it within a few months.
6.I describe myself with phrases like "I'm bad with money" or "I'm not a numbers person."
7.When I have more money than usual, it feels unfamiliar and tends to disappear.
8.I put other people's financial needs ahead of building my own foundation.
9.I spend money to change how I feel.
10.I make purchases after stressful, lonely, or exhausting moments that I later regret.
11.I can name the feeling behind most of my unplanned purchases.
12.I feel a rush while shopping that fades soon after I buy.
13.Money moves to savings automatically before I have a chance to spend it.
14.I save whatever is left at the end of the month, if anything is left.
15.I have a written plan for where my money goes each month.
16.My financial progress depends on how motivated I feel that month.

Part 3: Written Root Analysis

Your words are the most important part of this assessment. They're included in your report and sent to Darrell.

Write the first thing that comes to mind, then explain where you think that belief came from. Minimum 30 words.

Be specific with amounts and timelines. Minimum 30 words.

Connect it to the Stagnation Equation, your money identity, or your emotional drivers. Minimum 40 words.

When you submit, your personal report appears on this page and a copy is sent to Darrell Thompson. Take your time. Honest answers produce an accurate report. There are no right answers in the diagnostic sections, only true ones.
